Why BVI documents get apostilled at all

A British Virgin Islands company is incorporated under BVI law, and its records mean nothing abroad until someone independent vouches for the signature on them. Under the 1961 Hague Convention an apostille does exactly that, and once it is attached the document is accepted in every one of the 120-plus member states without any further embassy or consular legalisation.

In practice the demand comes from banks. Correspondent banking has tightened steadily, and an international bank opening an account for a BVI entity will now routinely require apostilled constitutional documents, apostilled evidence of good standing, and apostilled confirmation of who the directors and beneficial owners are. The same requirement appears in redomiciliation, in cross-border security and in litigation where the company's standing has to be proved to a foreign court.

The documents we handle

The two mistakes that cost weeks

The first is jurisdiction. A BVI document must be apostilled by the competent authority in the British Virgin Islands. Seychelles cannot apostille it, and no amount of certification here changes that. What we do is coordinate the BVI end through the registered agent and act as your single point of contact — which matters when the alternative is running an agent, a notary and a courier across three time zones yourself.

The second is staleness. A certificate of good standing has a shelf life in the eyes of a compliance officer — often three months, sometimes one. Legalising a certificate that was issued six months ago is money spent on a document that will be rejected on sight. We check the date against the receiving institution's requirement before anything is lodged, and re-order where the certificate will not survive the review.

Private documents need a notary first

Certificates issued by the BVI Registry are public documents and can be apostilled as they stand. A board resolution, an incumbency certificate signed by the registered agent, or a power of attorney is a private document: it has to be executed before or certified by a notary public first, and it is the notary's signature that the apostille then authenticates. Getting this the wrong way round is the commonest cause of a rejected pack.
