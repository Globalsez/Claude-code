Background

What an apostille actually is

An apostille is a standardised certificate, in a form fixed by the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961 Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public Documents. It is attached to a public document by a designated authority in the country where that document was issued, and it certifies the authenticity of the signature, the capacity in which the signatory acted, and the identity of the seal or stamp on the document.

What it does not do is certify the contents. An apostille on a certificate of good standing confirms that the Registrar's signature is genuine. It says nothing about whether the company is a good counterparty. Receiving institutions know this, which is why the underlying document still has to be the right one.

Who issues apostilles in Seychelles

Seychelles has been a party to the Convention since 1979. The competent authority for documents issued in Seychelles is the Supreme Court of Seychelles, at Ile du Port, Victoria. Apostilles are issued there against a fixed statutory fee, on the Court's published submission days, and documents are collected in person. Our role is to make sure what is lodged will be accepted, and to do the attending for you.

Which documents can be apostilled

Public documents — those issued by a public authority or bearing an official seal — can be apostilled directly. That covers civil status certificates, court documents, and records issued by the Registrar of Companies.

Private documents cannot. A commercial contract, a board resolution, a company letter or a power of attorney drawn up between private parties has to be turned into a document bearing an official act first — normally by being executed before, or certified by, a notary. The notary's signature is then what the apostille authenticates. This is the single most common reason a document is turned away, and it is entirely avoidable.

Documents issued outside Seychelles

A document must be apostilled by the competent authority of the country that issued it. Seychelles cannot apostille a British Virgin Islands certificate of incorporation, and the BVI cannot apostille a Seychelles birth certificate. Where an instruction spans jurisdictions we coordinate the whole set and remain your single point of contact, so you are not managing three service providers in three time zones.

What we need from you

  • A photograph or scan of the document, so we can confirm its form before anything is posted.
  • The country it is being sent to, and what it is for — this decides whether a translation or a further step is needed.
  • The original, where the document is one the Court requires in original form. We will tell you before you send anything.
  • A delivery address for the original once the apostille is issued.

Translations

Some receiving authorities require a certified translation, and some require the translation itself to be legalised. The order matters: translating after the apostille is issued is usually wrong, because the apostille then sits on a document the receiving authority cannot read. Tell us the destination and we will sequence it correctly.
